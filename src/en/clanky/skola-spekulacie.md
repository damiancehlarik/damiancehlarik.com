School of Speculation is a programme Damián Cehlárik led as part of Bratislava Design Week. Design here is not used to solve problems but to ask questions: it makes things from possible futures in order to look at the present differently. The theme was value, money and payment.

The first part draws on the speculative design of Anthony Dunne and Fiona Raby. Their A/B manifesto sets design that finds and questions problems against design that solves them. Projects such as United Micro Kingdoms and Foragers show that speculation does not begin with fantasy but with people who already live at the margins today. Participants therefore look for a topic through what troubles them rather than what they would like to improve.

The second part works with the design fiction of Julian Bleecker and the Near Future Laboratory: artefacts that look as if they already existed. It ranges from William Whyte's observation of the street through ubiquitous computing to the Audio Tooth Implant by James Auger and Jimmy Loizeau. Following the Work Kit of Design Fiction, participants assemble their own brief from an archetype, an object, an attribute and an action.

The third part moves to experiential futures, futures that can be lived through. It draws on Fred Polak, on the Three Horizons of Hodgson and Sharpe and on the work of Extrapolation Factory, which placed speculative products in an ordinary shop. The final brief reads: build a world, not an object. First the conditions of the world, then one situation from life within it, and only at the end an artefact that would feel entirely natural there.
