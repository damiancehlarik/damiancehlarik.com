Damian Works is a studio that Damián Cehlárik has run for ten years. Design there does not emerge as an authorial gesture but together with others: with clients, craftspeople, institutions and the people it is designed for.

The studio works with the concept of post-authorship. The concept builds on Barthes's death of the author and Foucault's question of what an author is, which digital practice has turned from theory into everyday experience. In the digital world authorship fragments: a design passes through shared files, templates, versions and tools that each leave their mark, and it can rarely be attributed to a single person. Damian Works does not hide this fragmentation but turns it into a method. Instead of a signature, it shows where and with whom a form came into being.

The studio's most regular institutional partner is the Metropolitan Institute of Bratislava (MIB), with whom it designs public space projects. For the competition on the future of Zimný prístav, the city's winter harbour, it designed exhibitions and awards. At TU-BA, the first centre for architecture in Slovakia, it created the opening exhibition Do You Know Bratislava? and, together with MIB, is turning the centre into a multifunctional exhibition space.

Articles about the studio's work are therefore written differently from a portfolio. They give full credits, describe the process and leave room for the voices of partners. The studio's portfolio is being prepared at damian.works.
